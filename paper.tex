\begin{abstract}
We study how a token's residual-stream representation moves, not just where it ends up,
across the depth of a language model generating a chain-of-thought (CoT) trace. For each
token we build a label-free \emph{fingerprint}: the concatenation of its per-layer
residual-stream deltas across every transition in the network. This object has a
well-defined geometry that is far from a random walk -- population-median
\emph{tortuosity} (path length over net displacement) of 1.84--2.02 across six task
families, against a $\sqrt{27}\approx5.2$ isotropic random-walk baseline at the same
depth -- and that geometry is not uniform across token roles.
\texttt{<think>}/\texttt{</think>} tokens show a distinctive, reproducible archetype
(tortuosity 2.62--2.70, no exceptions across all six datasets; a multi-lag directional
persistence that never fully decays) that we term \emph{wanders and returns}, distinct
from tokens that simply drift with the population. Independent component analysis
recovers a dedicated \texttt{<think>} component in every dataset we test. We then ask
whether this geometry is more than descriptive: does it predict which layers are
\emph{causally} load-bearing? At the single-layer level, no -- geometric magnitude and
reorientation are weak, non-significant predictors of causal importance, even after
correcting for a specific confound (generic-perturbation vulnerability) that a naive
read of the correlation would miss. But we show this null is a validity problem rather
than a clean answer: the single-layer metric itself fails to detect the one causal
effect this paper independently establishes three other ways (individually ranking
21st/25th/26th of 28 layers), so its non-correlation with geometry is not yet
informative either way. At the \emph{group} level, though, we find
a real, controlled effect: three consecutive layers that are individually
low-importance and individually redundant are jointly necessary -- bypassing them
collapses the trace's answer while reasoning stays coherent, and this effect survives a
permutation-based control (injecting another token's real, same-layer activity rather
than isotropic noise) that rules out the most obvious alternative explanation. We report
this net result plainly: representational geometry does not predict causal importance
locally, but a joint-necessity structure exists and reproduces under stress-testing.
Separately, we show sentence-category probes of CoT (the standard tool for asking
whether internal state tracks reasoning structure) are substantially confounded by
sequence position -- a label carrying no semantic content recovers 70--79\% of a real
category probe's mutual information on five of six datasets -- and quantify what
survives proper control. We report, rather than suppress, a null result on our own
original methodological premise (tracking representational \emph{change} versus raw
\emph{state}): our best-powered dataset favors state, and we treat the trajectory-geometry
object above as useful on its own empirical merits, not because deltas have been shown to
beat state at a downstream task. \todo{Second-model replication of the geometry results
(Qwen3-8B and Phi-4-mini-reasoning generation is complete or in flight; Open gap 8
analysis on either has not yet been run) and MATH-benchmark integration are in progress
and not yet reflected in the numbers above -- see \Cref{sec:limitations}.}
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

A growing body of work studies large language models' chain-of-thought (CoT) traces not
as opaque text but as a sequence of functionally distinct steps -- \emph{problem setup},
\emph{fact retrieval}, \emph{active computation}, \emph{result consolidation}, and so on
\citep{wei2022cot,thoughtanchors2025}. Almost all of this work asks what a token's
representation encodes \emph{at} a layer -- a static, per-layer question. We ask a
different question: what does the \emph{path} a token's representation takes across
depth look like, and does that path carry information a single snapshot cannot?

We build a purely representational, label-free per-token object -- a \emph{fingerprint}
(\Cref{sec:methods:fingerprint}) formed from the residual-stream delta at every layer
transition -- and characterize its geometry directly. Two things are true of this
geometry that, to our knowledge, have not been reported before. First, at the population
level, these paths are far straighter than chance: median tortuosity (path length over
net displacement) of 1.84--2.02 against an isotropic random-walk baseline of
$\sqrt{27}\approx5.2$, holding across six structurally different task families with no
exceptions. Second, this straightness is not uniform: \texttt{<think>}/\texttt{</think>}
tokens systematically deviate from the population in a specific, reproducible way -- a
\emph{wanders and returns} archetype, elevated tortuosity paired with strong growth in
representational distinctiveness across depth -- while a multi-lag directional analysis
rules out two simpler alternative descriptions of the same phenomenon (pure
position-noise, and period-2 alternation).

Having established that trajectory geometry is a real, reproducible object, we ask
whether it is more than descriptive. \textbf{Does per-layer geometry predict per-layer
causal importance?} We measure two geometric quantities at every layer -- update
magnitude and reorientation (the cosine between successive deltas) -- and correlate each
against a causal-importance ground truth built from a matched-magnitude bypass-versus-injection
design (\Cref{sec:methods:causalgeom}). At the single-layer level the answer is no: neither
predictor is significant, even after we correct the causal target for a specific
confound (a layer's vulnerability to a \emph{generic}, content-free perturbation of the
same size) that a naive reading of an earlier, unplanned correlation would have missed.
But single-layer causal importance itself turns out to be a poor instrument here: three
consecutive layers that individually rank near the bottom of all layers by causal
importance are, together, strictly necessary -- removing all three collapses the CoT
trace's final answer while reasoning stays coherent, an effect that survives replacing
isotropic-noise injection with a harder, same-subspace permutation control (injecting
another token's own observed activity at the same layer). Geometry does not predict this
joint effect either, at the resolution we can currently measure it -- consistent with,
though not proof of, a broader pattern in this project that representational
distinctiveness and causal importance are different axes.

Separately from the geometry results, we audit the standard tool this line of work uses
to ask whether internal state tracks CoT structure at all: a \emph{sentence-category
probe}, which pools a sentence's activations and predicts a functional category label.
We show this style of probe is confounded by sequence position -- the category taxonomy
used throughout this line of work is quasi-ordinal by construction, so a label carrying
no semantic content whatsoever already recovers most of a real probe's signal -- verify
this confound is present, unaddressed, in the leading published result of this kind, and
quantify what genuine content signal survives a proper control
(\Cref{sec:results:position}). We report this as a supporting methodological
contribution, not the paper's headline: it establishes what a category-probe number can
and cannot be trusted to mean, and motivates why we build a label-free alternative
(the fingerprint's geometry) as our primary instrument instead.

Finally, in the course of building the fingerprint object, we tested this project's
original methodological premise -- that tracking layer-to-layer \emph{change} is a
better probe substrate than tracking raw \emph{state} -- against the natural baseline
(clustering raw residual-stream activations instead of deltas). \textbf{We report this
honestly as unresolved}, and, having found no evidence for it, do not build the paper's
central claim on it: the geometric results above stand on their own empirical merits
(they describe a real, measured object), not on a prior claim that deltas are the
superior representational choice (\Cref{sec:limitations}).

\textbf{Contributions.}
\begin{enumerate}[topsep=1pt, partopsep=0pt, itemsep=0pt, leftmargin=0.7cm]
\renewcommand{\labelenumi}{(\theenumi)}
\item We introduce a label-free, per-token fingerprint object built from residual-stream
  deltas and characterize its geometry directly: paths are far straighter than an
  isotropic random walk at the population level (tortuosity 1.84--2.02 vs.\ $\sqrt{27}
  \approx 5.2$), with no exceptions across six task families.
\item We identify a reproducible, token-class-specific geometric archetype
  (\texttt{<think>}/\texttt{</think>}'s \emph{wanders and returns} signature, tortuosity
  2.62--2.70 with no exceptions) and pin its mechanism via a multi-lag directional
  analysis that rules out two simpler alternative explanations.
\item We test whether this geometry predicts causal importance. At the single-layer
  level it does not, a null we verify is not an artifact of an uncontrolled causal
  target. At the group level, we find and stress-test a real joint-necessity effect
  that geometry also does not predict at our current resolution -- a result we report as
  a genuine, checked null rather than a failure to find the right transformation of the
  data.
\item We show sentence-category CoT probes are confounded by sequence position,
  quantify how much of an uncontrolled probe's signal this accounts for (70--79\% of AMI
  on five of six datasets), verify the confound is present, unaddressed, in the leading
  published result of this kind, and quantify what survives proper control.
\item We report, rather than suppress, a null result on our own founding premise (delta
  vs.\ state) and an explicit accounting of what currently blocks a fully general claim
  (\Cref{sec:limitations}).
\end{enumerate}

\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}

\textbf{Representational geometry and dynamics.} Most mechanistic-interpretability work
treats a layer's activation as the unit of analysis, asking what it encodes
\citep{alain2016probing,belinkov2022probing}. We instead treat the \emph{path} a
representation takes across layers as the unit of analysis, following the general
intuition that a function is characterized by both its value and its rate of change; to
our knowledge this specific object (a per-token, whole-depth, delta-based path summarized
by simple geometric statistics) and its link to causal importance have not been
previously reported.

\textbf{Probing and its limits.} A long line of work cautions that probe accuracy alone
does not establish that a model \emph{uses} the probed information
\citep{alain2016probing,belinkov2022probing} -- a probe can succeed by recovering
information latent in the input rather than information the model's computation depends
on. Our position-confound result (\Cref{sec:results:position}) is a specific, structural
instance of this general caution, applied to a taxonomy whose categories are correlated
with an easily-recoverable nuisance variable (sequence position) by construction.

\textbf{Chain-of-thought and thought anchors.} \citet{wei2022cot} popularized eliciting
step-by-step reasoning traces from language models. \citet{thoughtanchors2025} propose
analyzing such traces at sentence granularity, introducing an 8-category functional
taxonomy and two measurement tools we build on: a resampling-based causal importance
metric, and a sentence-category probe on pooled residual-stream activations. We audit the
latter directly (\Cref{sec:results:position}) and adopt an attention-suppression variant
of the former (\Cref{sec:methods:causal}).

\textbf{Layer-wise relevance propagation for transformers.} LRP \citep{bach2015lrp}
attributes a model's output back through its computation graph. \citet{ali2022xai}
(CP-LRP) and \citet{achtibat2024attnlrp} (AttnLRP) both adapt LRP to transformer
attention; our attribution pipeline follows the older, simpler CP-LRP convention (value
receives all relevance, query/key receive none) rather than AttnLRP's gradient-split
rule, a choice we revisit in \Cref{sec:methods:lrp}. We build on
\citet{nanda2022transformerlens} for model internals access.

\textbf{Successor and ordinal circuits.} \citet{gould2024successor} identify
\emph{successor heads}: attention heads that specialize across many models and ordinal
sequence types (days, months, numerals) in advancing a sequence by one step. Our
successor-circuit result (\Cref{sec:results:successor}) is a direct, model-internal test
of whether their claimed cross-category generality holds when the circuit is
characterized mechanistically (via attribution) rather than only behaviorally, on task
families we generate ourselves.

\section{Methods}
\label{sec:methods}

\subsection{Task datasets and generation regime}
\label{sec:methods:tasks}

We use six synthetic reasoning tasks, each eliciting an explicit CoT trace via the
\texttt{<think>}/\texttt{</think>} convention: \textsc{alphabet} and its disjoint variant
\textsc{alphabet\_disjoint} (recite the next $k$ letters from a given starting letter),
\textsc{days\_of\_week} (an analogous successor task over day names), \textsc{numbers\_letters}
(a joint numeral/letter successor task), \textsc{countries\_capitals} (factual recall),
and \textsc{ioi} (indirect-object identification, a name-tracking/coreference task). This
spans ordinal-successor tasks, a factual-recall task, and a coreference task -- three
qualitatively different demands on the model, not six variations of one task. All
reported numbers use Qwen3-0.6B \citep{qwen3}, 28 layers.
\todo{MATH \citep{hendrycks2021math} integration is in progress; see \Cref{sec:math} and
\Cref{sec:limitations}.}

All representational and relevance data reported here come from \textbf{greedy}
decoding, generated with a custom generation loop that preserves gradient flow back
through every layer for LRP (standard \texttt{HookedTransformer.generate} drops backward
hooks past the embedding layer). Causal-importance measurements
(\Cref{sec:methods:causal,sec:methods:causalgeom}) instead use \textbf{teacher-forced}
forward passes over the same already-generated greedy sequence, with plain forward hooks
(no backward pass, no relevance signal needed) rather than a fresh generation loop.
Sampled decoding (temperature 0.6) is reserved for a separate, in-progress extension to
the MATH dataset (\Cref{sec:math}), not used for any causal-importance number reported in
this draft.

\subsection{The fingerprint object}
\label{sec:methods:fingerprint}

For each token $t$ at each layer transition $\ell \to \ell{+}1$, we compute the
residual-stream delta $\delta_t^\ell = h_t^{\ell+1} - h_t^\ell$. Following $L{-}1$
independently-fit Gaussian random projections \citep{bingham2001random} (one per
transition, $n_{\text{components}}=32$), we concatenate the projected deltas across all
layer transitions into a single per-token vector -- a \emph{fingerprint} summarizing how
a token's representation evolved across the \emph{entire} depth of the network, not its
state at any one layer. This object is entirely label-free: it never sees a category
label during construction, only token identities and raw activations. For
\Cref{sec:results:cluster}'s clustering-based validity checks we further project the
fingerprint matrix with t-SNE \citep{vandermaaten2008tsne} and cluster it with spectral
clustering \citep{ng2001spectral} (nearest-neighbors affinity; neighbor count validated
per dataset); for \Cref{sec:methods:geometry}'s geometric characterization we instead
work with the raw per-layer delta sequence directly, with no clustering step.

\textbf{Clustering validity.} Two token classes cluster reliably across a five-fold sweep
of the cluster count $k$ (8--80), a change of random-projection seed, and four
independent datasets: \emph{meta} tokens (\texttt{user}/\texttt{wants}/\texttt{asked})
and \emph{noise} tokens (\texttt{\textbackslash n}, \texttt{<|endoftext|>},
\texttt{<|im\_end|>}); noise reaches perfect precision at every $k$ in every dataset
tested. An earlier reading of three distinct letter subgroups and a dedicated discourse
cluster did \emph{not} survive the same sweep -- both were artifacts of a single blob
being sliced arbitrarily finer as $k$ grows, not real substructure, and we report this
retraction explicitly rather than silently dropping the earlier claim. At a finer
granularity, per-(token, layer-transition) clusters (rather than the concatenated
fingerprint) are 89--93\% single-transition-specific across a $5\times$ range of $k$
(25--120) -- tokens mostly do not jump between clusters as depth increases, they occupy
one characteristic transition-specific niche. \textbf{Cross-model check:} on Qwen3-8B
(13$\times$ larger, 36 vs.\ 28 layers), the identical sweep gives 95.0--99.2\%
single-transition specificity on two further task families (\textsc{days\_of\_week},
\textsc{numbers\_letters}) -- above the entire 89--93\% band the primary model produces,
not merely within it.

\textbf{Necessity.} We separately test whether the layers most associated with this
structure are behaviorally load-bearing, via hard (zero-)ablation of layers 24--26 (as
opposed to the softer clamping used in earlier pilot experiments, which only shows
tolerance, not necessity). Three independent signals agree: behaviorally, the CoT trace
itself stays coherent but the answer immediately following it collapses into repetition;
representationally, the otherwise-always-pure noise cluster merges into an
undifferentiated garbage cluster; and via a logit-lens \emph{next-token prediction
accuracy} check (computed by projecting each layer's residual stream through the
unembedding matrix and checking whether it recovers the token that immediately follows
it), accuracy at the deepest layers drops from 89\% in a clean run to $\sim$11\% under
ablation. (An earlier version of this analysis described this metric as
"self-recoverability" -- whether a layer's own token identity remains linearly decodable
-- but a data-alignment audit established the metric, as implemented, is in fact
next-token prediction accuracy; the 89\%/11\% numbers are unchanged, and we consider the
corrected description, "ablating layers 24--26 destroys next-token prediction," a more
direct claim than the original.) A full 28-layer bypass instead yields \emph{zero}
relevance anywhere in the network -- a structural consequence of how bypassing severs the
backward graph, not a bug -- so this comparison is only possible at the partial-ablation
depth.

\subsection{Trajectory geometry}
\label{sec:methods:geometry}

We characterize the raw per-layer delta sequence $\{\delta_t^1, \dots, \delta_t^{L-1}\}$
of each token directly, with three statistics.

\textbf{Tortuosity.} $\text{tort}(t) = \frac{\sum_\ell \|\delta_t^\ell\|}{\|\sum_\ell
\delta_t^\ell\|}$, the ratio of total path length to net displacement -- 1 for a
perfectly straight path, growing without bound for a path that wanders and returns
toward its starting region. We compare the observed population median against a
simulated isotropic random walk of the same dimensionality and step count ($\sqrt{27}
\approx 5.196$ for our 28-layer model's 27 transitions).

\textbf{Multi-lag directional persistence.} $\cos(\delta_t^{\ell}, \delta_t^{\ell+k})$
for lag $k \in \{1,2,3,\dots,10\}$, averaged over tokens and layers. We use this rather
than lag-1 cosine alone (a standard "linearity" metric) after establishing that lag 1 is
specifically corrupted by position-level jitter decorrelation (\Cref{sec:results:geometry}) and
does not represent the trajectory's true directional persistence.

\textbf{Growing distinctness.} We z-score each token's per-layer distance from its
population centroid and correlate this z-score against layer depth, giving a per-token
"growth-correlation" -- whether a token becomes progressively more representationally
distinct as depth increases.

We additionally decompose the fingerprint matrix with independent component analysis
\citep{hyvarinen2000ica}, motivated by principal component analysis (PCA) visibly
blending multiple token-class signals into shared axes; ICA components are inspected for
token-class purity rather than used as a downstream classifier.

\subsection{LRP conservation and rule choices}
\label{sec:methods:lrp}

We verify, rather than assume, that our LRP implementation satisfies exact relevance
conservation. For our primary model (Qwen3-0.6B, 28 layers), every one of 29 conservation
checkpoints (the embedding layer plus all 28 residual-stream pre-layers) lands at a ratio
of exactly $1.0000$ (float32 precision) against the model's own zero-bias architecture,
under detach-based rules (\texttt{ln-rule-pre}, \texttt{ah-rule}, \texttt{half-rule-mlp},
\texttt{identity-rule-act}) that make every genuinely nonlinear operation exactly
homogeneous of degree 1 during backpropagation, so Euler's homogeneous function theorem
guarantees exact conservation without further assumptions. The same mechanism transfers
to a second model (Apertus-8B \citep{apertus}, 32 layers, 33 checkpoints): float32 ratios
land in $0.999857$--$1.000000$, converging monotonically to exactly $1.0$ at the later
layers -- a small residual spread we attribute to rounding rather than a rule mismatch,
since a bfloat16 run of the identical check on the same model shows a much wider
$0.9975$--$1.0037$ spread with no systematic drift by depth, the expected signature of
precision noise scaling with the numeric format rather than a structural gap. A third
model, Phi-4-mini-reasoning (fused gate/up MLP projection, requiring a separate
bridge-based loading path since it uses a rotary-embedding scaling scheme our primary
tooling does not implement), also reaches exact conservation ($0.99998$) once the
corresponding MLP-activation rule is extended to its fused-projection architecture.
We follow the older, simpler CP-LRP attention rule \citep{ali2022xai} (query and key
receive 0\% relevance, value receives 100\%) rather than AttnLRP's newer gradient-split
rule \citep{achtibat2024attnlrp} (25\%/25\%/50\%); we flag this as a deliberate,
non-urgent methodological choice point rather than an error, since both are defensible
and our conservation result does not distinguish between them.

\subsection{Position-only control and conditional AMI}
\label{sec:methods:position}

We label every token position in every trace using Thought Anchors' 8-category taxonomy
\citep{thoughtanchors2025} (\texttt{problem\_setup}, \texttt{fact\_retrieval},
\texttt{active\_computation}, \texttt{result\_consolidation},
\texttt{final\_answer\_emission}, and three further categories), assigned by an LLM
annotator (see \Cref{sec:limitations} for the resulting caveat). We measure how well
unsupervised fingerprint clusters agree with these category labels using adjusted mutual
information (AMI) \citep{vinh2010ami}, which corrects for the chance agreement inflation
that a naive purity metric is vulnerable to as cluster count grows.

To test for a position confound, we construct a content-free control label
$\text{pos}_i \in \{0, 1, \dots\}$ recording only a token's normalized rank within its
sentence's trace -- no semantic content whatsoever -- and compute
$\text{AMI}(\text{cluster}, \text{pos})$ identically to
$\text{AMI}(\text{cluster}, \text{category})$. To measure what survives once position is
held fixed, we compute a \emph{conditional} statistic: we bin tokens into $B$
quantile-based position strata, compute AMI(cluster, category) independently within each
stratum, and aggregate (size-weighted) across strata -- an empirical approximation to
$I(\text{cluster}; \text{category} \mid \text{position})$, not the formal conditional
mutual information estimator. We sweep $B \in \{4, 8, 16, 32\}$, since a single bin count
is exactly the kind of unswept parameter our own earlier analysis warns against trusting.

\subsection{Causal importance: attention suppression}
\label{sec:methods:causal}

We measure a sentence's causal importance with a metric adapted from, but not identical
to, \citet{thoughtanchors2025}: suppress attention \emph{to} a sentence's token span
(masking the relevant \texttt{hook\_attn\_scores} entries pre-softmax at every layer,
verified directly to zero out post-softmax attention weight in the masked region while
leaving other rows correctly normalized) and measure the resulting KL divergence between
the suppressed and unperturbed \emph{local next-token} logit distributions at each
downstream position, averaged per target sentence -- not, as in the original paper, a
resampling-based estimate over full downstream answer distributions. We use this local
proxy because our tasks' near-deterministic answers give the paper's own resampling
metric little outcome variance to measure; both are evaluated via teacher-forced forward
passes over the greedy trace (\Cref{sec:methods:tasks}), not sampled decoding.

\subsection{Causal importance: layer-level bypass and matched-magnitude injection}
\label{sec:methods:causalgeom}

To test whether trajectory geometry predicts \emph{per-layer} causal importance, we
build a second, finer-grained causal ground truth. For each layer $\ell$ and each
sample, we compare two conditions of identical perturbation magnitude at the model's
final-answer span: \textbf{(A, bypass)} replace layer $\ell$'s output with its input
(removing $\delta^\ell$ entirely); \textbf{(B, injection)} leave $\delta^\ell$ in place
and additively inject a perturbation of the same per-token norm, sourced two ways --
(B1) an isotropic random direction, and (B2), a harder control, another token's own
observed $\delta^\ell$ at the same layer, direction-normalized and rescaled to the
target token's norm. B2 shares the perturbation's real activation statistics and
effective subspace with the treatment condition, differing only in whose computation
produced it, and is designed to rule out the specific alternative explanation that an
isotropic direction is simply too far outside the residual stream's effective subspace
to constitute a fair comparison. We measure KL divergence between clean and perturbed
output distributions at the answer span, identically to \Cref{sec:methods:causal}. We
apply this design at both the single-layer level (all 28 layers swept) and, for the
specific three-layer block identified as behaviorally load-bearing in
\Cref{sec:methods:fingerprint}, at the pairwise and triple-combination level.

\subsection{State-vs-delta baseline}
\label{sec:methods:statevsdelta}

To test whether tracking change (deltas, \Cref{sec:methods:fingerprint}) is actually
necessary for the clustering-based validity checks above -- as opposed to raw per-layer
state carrying the same information -- we build a \emph{state fingerprint} using the
identical projection and clustering pipeline, but applied to raw \texttt{resid\_pre}
activations instead of layer-transition deltas, and compare AMI against the same
category labels. We sweep the neighbor-count parameter of the spectral affinity graph
symmetrically on \emph{both} the state and delta sides (5, 10, 15, 20, 25, 30, 40) rather
than fixing one side to whatever was already on disk, after an earlier, asymmetric
version of this comparison overstated the delta side's case (see
\Cref{sec:limitations}). This test targets only the clustering object's ability to
recover a category label, not the geometric characterization of
\Cref{sec:methods:geometry}, which has no state-only analogue: raw per-layer state has
no notion of tortuosity or directional persistence, since those are properties of a
\emph{path}, not a point.

\section{Results}
\label{sec:results}

\subsection{Trajectory geometry is a real, non-random object}
\label{sec:results:geometry}

\Cref{tab:tortuosity} reports population-median tortuosity across all six datasets
against the isotropic random-walk baseline. Every dataset lands well below the
random-walk value, with a tight cross-dataset range (1.84--2.02) despite the six task
families spanning ordinal-successor, factual-recall, and coreference demands -- paths
are considerably straighter than chance, a result we consider underrated relative to the
token-class-specific findings that follow, since it establishes the geometric object
itself is meaningfully structured before asking whether any single token class deviates
from it.

\textbf{A token-class-specific archetype.} \texttt{<think>}/\texttt{</think>} tokens
show elevated tortuosity (2.62--2.70) in \emph{every} dataset without exception, paired
with the tightest cross-dataset statistic in this paper: growth-correlation of
0.90--0.94 for \texttt{</think>} (representational distinctiveness from the population
centroid increases essentially monotonically with depth). We term this combination
-- large total path length relative to net displacement, together with steadily growing
distinctiveness -- \emph{wanders and returns}: the token's representation does not settle
early and drift, it actively explores before its final position stabilizes.
\texttt{noise} tokens (\texttt{\textbackslash n}, \texttt{<|endoftext|>}) show a smaller,
still-consistent elevation (2.30--2.37); no token class we test occupies the opposite
extreme (tortuosity $\approx1$, a "moves far, straight" pattern), a clean constraining
negative.

\textbf{Resolving an apparent inconsistency.} A lag-1-only directional statistic
(cosine between consecutive deltas) is weak and close to zero population-wide
(0.036--0.073), which in isolation could be misread as "trajectories are close to a
random walk in direction" -- apparently in tension with the tortuosity result above. A
multi-lag sweep resolves this: cosine rises from lag 1 to a plateau at lags 2--3
(0.107--0.153), then decays slowly without reaching zero by lag 10 (0.072--0.088).
Simulating three candidate generating processes shows lag 1 specifically, and only lag 1,
is suppressed by position-level jitter: pure position-noise-with-differencing predicts
lag-1 $\approx -0.5$ (not observed); period-2 alternation predicts lag-3 $\approx 0$ with
lag-10 elevated (the opposite of the observed shape). The data is best explained by a
composite process -- drift-plus-step-noise setting the overall level, a modest amount of
position-noise specifically suppressing lag 1, and genuine path curvature driving the
slow decay at longer lags. We retract the "close to a random walk" reading and adopt a
calibrated one: peak persistence ($\approx0.13$) is far below what a genuinely smooth
path would show (simulated: 0.9+ at short lags), so the accurate description is a
\textbf{mostly-diffusive path carrying a weak but persistent directional component} --
neither "smooth trajectory" nor "random walk" is accurate on its own.

\textbf{Independent components separate what PCA blends.} A principal-component
projection of the fingerprint matrix visibly mixes multiple token-class signals into
shared axes; ICA \citep{hyvarinen2000ica} cleanly separates them. \texttt{<think>} tokens
receive a dedicated independent component in \emph{every one of six datasets} we test --
the single most robust finding in this line of analysis. Discourse-marker tokens
(\texttt{Let}/\texttt{So}/\texttt{But}-family words) share a distinct component with
partial word-membership overlap, replicated across five of six datasets.

\subsection{Trajectory geometry does not predict single-layer causal importance --
but joint necessity is real and geometry does not predict that either}
\label{sec:results:causalgeom}

We first tested whether the two natural per-layer geometric summaries --
$P_1(\ell) = \text{mean}_i\|\delta_i^\ell\|$ (magnitude) and $P_2(\ell) =
\text{mean}_i\cos(\delta_i^{\ell-1},\delta_i^\ell)$ (reorientation) -- predict per-layer
causal importance (\Cref{sec:methods:causalgeom}'s bypass condition, swept across all 28
layers). Neither correlates significantly with raw bypass-KL (Spearman $\rho=-0.207$,
$p=0.29$ for $P_1$; $\rho=0.023$, $p=0.91$ for $P_2$; $n=28$ layers, the correct unit of
analysis since per-layer values are already aggregated across samples).

\textbf{A validity problem, not a null result.} Layers 24--26, which
\Cref{sec:methods:fingerprint} independently establishes as behaviorally load-bearing as
a \emph{group}, individually rank 21st, 25th, and 26th of 28 by single-layer bypass-KL --
near the bottom, not the top. The single-layer causal metric fails to detect the one
causal effect this paper independently establishes three other ways
(\Cref{sec:methods:fingerprint}), so a correlation against it is not yet informative
about the geometric predictors either way.

\textbf{Testing joint necessity directly.} We bypass layers 24, 25, and 26 in
combination (pairs and the full triple) and compare against the matched-magnitude
injection controls of \Cref{sec:methods:causalgeom}. \Cref{tab:jointnecessity} reports
the triple: bypass produces far more damage than either injection control, in
\emph{every one of 30 measured samples}, including the harder same-subspace permutation
control (B2). The isotropic control (B1) somewhat overstates the effect (ratio
6.9$\times$ mean) relative to the harder, same-subspace control (B2, ratio 5.1$\times$
mean, 7.1$\times$ median) -- consistent with isotropic noise being a partially weaker
perturbation than same-subspace content, as one would expect if the residual stream
occupies a lower-effective-dimensional subspace than its ambient dimensionality -- but
the qualitative result (bypass $\gg$ injection) survives the harder control decisively
($p=1.9\times10^{-9}$, Wilcoxon, $n=30$).

This rules out the two competing readings that would predict bypass $\approx$ injection
(generic capacity/unrecoverability) or bypass $\ll$ injection (redundancy narrowly tuned
to the model's own computation) -- the specific computation these three layers perform
together matters, not just how much representational capacity is removed.

\textbf{Does geometry predict this group-level effect?} We correlate $P_1$/$P_2$ against
a corrected single-layer causal target -- bypass-KL in excess of a matched-magnitude
generic injection (the same logic as \Cref{tab:jointnecessity}, applied per layer rather
than per combination -- addresses the possibility that the original null above was an
artifact of an uncorrected causal target. It is not: neither $P_1$ ($\rho=-0.101$,
$p=0.61$) nor $P_2$ ($\rho=0.164$, $p=0.42$) is significant, and layers 24--26 remain in
the bottom third by rank (19th/20th/23rd of 28) even under this corrected target. We read
this as a genuine null rather than a failure to find the right transformation of the
data: per-layer geometric summaries do not predict a joint, interactional causal
property, which is consistent with -- though does not prove -- a broader pattern in this
line of work that representational distinctiveness and causal importance are simply
different axes (\Cref{sec:results:causal}).

\textbf{An exploratory side-observation, checked and partially retracted.} We noticed
$P_1$ and $P_2$ each significantly predict a layer's vulnerability to a \emph{generic}
injection alone ($P_1$: $\rho=-0.39$, $p=0.04$; $P_2$: $\rho=-0.53$, $p=0.004$). Checking
whether this reflects a real magnitude/reorientation effect or a confound (injected
magnitude is matched to $\|\delta^\ell\|$ in absolute terms, while the residual stream's
own norm grows substantially with depth in our model, more than 50-fold from the first
to the deepest layers), we find $P_1$'s correlation collapses to noise once normalized by
the ambient residual norm ($\rho=-0.006$, $p=0.98$) -- a norm-scaling artifact, which we
retract. $P_2$'s correlation is unaffected by this specific check, since cosine
similarity is already scale-invariant by construction, and we report it as it stands:
real, but unplanned, exploratory, and not yet replicated on any other dataset.

\subsection{Representational distinctiveness and causal importance are different axes}
\label{sec:results:causal}

For each measured sentence, we take its dominant fingerprint cluster (at $k{=}60$) and

\subsection{A successor circuit generalizes across ordinal categories, but relocates in
depth}
\label{sec:results:successor}

We first characterize, via direct attribution inspection (not clustering, which mostly
fails the same substructure-artifact check as \Cref{sec:methods:fingerprint}'s retracted
letter-subgroup claim), a four-phase attribution pattern in letter-recitation traces:
attention-sink $\to$ semantic anchor $\to$ \emph{immediately preceding letter in the
enumeration} (induction-like) $\to$ local punctuation. We confirm layer 16, head 14 is a
genuine synthetic induction head (score 0.974 on the standard diagnostic) -- but, as a
negative control, that head is \emph{not} the driver of the real letter-succession
pattern: on every real instance we check, it attributes to punctuation, not the
preceding letter. The real signal is distributed across a different head set
(13, 3, 7, 9, 8), with head 13 the sharpest single specialist (53\% of instances, layer
16).

We then test whether this mechanism transfers to a second ordinal category,
\textsc{days\_of\_week}, using the identical per-head, per-layer LRP-relevance argmax
method on all 81 day-name recitation instances across our sample. At the task's own peak
layer (26), the best single head reaches 54.3\% (44/81) -- nearly identical concentration
to letters' 53\%, and that head (head 9) is a member of the same candidate set letters
surfaced. The reading: a comparably sharp, single-head-dominated successor phenomenon
exists for both ordinal tasks, relocated in depth (16 vs.\ 26) with partial head overlap
-- positive evidence for \citet{gould2024successor}'s claim of cross-category
generality, with a genuine open question (sequence familiarity? token complexity?
training data frequency?) about why the depth shifts, which we leave for future work.

\subsection{Position confounds sentence-category probing (supporting result)}
\label{sec:results:position}

The trajectory-geometry results above are entirely label-free. This section audits the
alternative, label-dependent tool this line of work otherwise relies on --
sentence-category probing -- as a supporting methodological contribution, not because our
own headline results depend on it.

the real category probe's AMI, at $k{=}80$. Five of six datasets fall in a tight
0.70--0.79 band; \textsc{ioi} is the outlier at 0.44, plausibly because its short,
non-templated, procedurally varied samples give position far less to latch onto than the
other tasks' more regular structure.

This alone does not mean the real probe's signal is fake: category still adds real
information beyond position on every dataset (the ratio is $<1.0$ throughout). But
naively subtracting, $\text{AMI(category)} - \text{AMI(position)}$, to estimate ``the
real signal net of position'' is statistically invalid -- mutual information quantities
do not decompose additively -- and an earlier version of our own analysis made exactly
this error before we caught and corrected it. The properly conditional statistic
(\Cref{sec:methods:position}) is the right tool.

\textbf{Auditing the leading published result.} We verified directly from the LaTeX
source of \citet{thoughtanchors2025} (the compiled PDF truncates before the relevant
appendix, so we did not rely on it) that their reported macro-F1$=0.71$ sentence-category
probe uses mean-pooled late-layer residual activations, a multinomial logistic
regression, group-5-fold cross-validation by problem (preventing content leakage across
folds, but not position leakage), and balanced class weights -- with no position feature
and no position-only baseline. We searched their full compiled source for every mention
of position: it appears in exactly two places, both scoped to a different analysis
(their receiver-head/attention study), including their own explicit acknowledgment that
\emph{``[their] receiver-head analyses are confounded by a sentence's position in the
reasoning trace''} -- a statement never connected to the probe. We considered four
candidate defenses of their probe against our confound and found none sufficient:
sample-level (not position-level) cross-validation prevents content leakage but does
nothing for position; raw pooled activations carry position implicitly by construction;
balanced class weights address label imbalance, an orthogonal concern; and high
per-category accuracy across categories with different typical positions demonstrates
real content signal exists but is fully compatible with signal and the position confound
coexisting, which is exactly what we measure. We state this precisely rather than as a
gotcha: \citet{thoughtanchors2025} identify position confounding for one analysis in
their paper and do not extend the observation to their probe; we show it applies there
too, and quantify what survives.

\textbf{What survives proper control.} \Cref{tab:conditional-ami} sweeps stratification
granularity from 4 to 32 quantile bins. Retention decays substantially between 4 and 16

the single most trustworthy trend in the sweep: retention plateaus at $\approx$49\%, not
the 4-bin check's 77\%. The other five datasets thin to as few as 4--18 populated strata
by 32 bins, so their finest-grained figures are estimated from a shrinking,
unrepresentative subset rather than reliably measured.

\textbf{Reading.} Nothing collapses toward zero under proper control -- category signal
is genuinely not \emph{just} position -- but the honest effect size is roughly a third to

(rather than size-weighted within-bin AMI), continuous (non-binned) position as a

\textbf{Calibration against a permutation null.} Every raw AMI number reported in this
section (and in \Cref{sec:results:causal,sec:results:successor}'s category-dependent

category labels among labeled tokens 2000 times and recomputing AMI each time gives a null

dataset, with the observed value 13--261 standard deviations above it
($p<0.0005$ throughout, the resolution limit of 2000 permutations). This does not change
any reported AMI value -- it establishes that none of them are plausibly chance
clustering agreement.

\section{Limitations}
\label{sec:limitations}

We state these plainly rather than as a closing formality, since several are the
difference between a general claim and a scoped one.

\textbf{Second model: infrastructure is not the same as analysis.} Every geometric and
causal result above is on one model (Qwen3-0.6B). We have separately confirmed our LRP
implementation achieves exact relevance conservation on two further models (Apertus-8B
and Phi-4-mini-reasoning, \Cref{sec:methods:lrp}), and completed one full task run on
Apertus-8B, but that model does not produce \texttt{<think>}/\texttt{</think>} output on
any task we have tried it on -- a trained-behavior property, not a code defect, confirmed
on two structurally different tasks -- so only the population-level geometric baseline
(tortuosity) transfers to it; the token-class-specific results
(\Cref{sec:results:geometry}) cannot currently be tested on this model at all, in either
direction. A CoT-capable third model (Phi-4-mini-reasoning, via a separate loading path
since our primary tooling's rotary-position implementation does not support this model's
context-scaling scheme) and a second-scale run of our primary model (Qwen3-8B) both have
generation complete or in flight as of this writing. \todo{Qwen3-8B now has Open gap 8's
geometric analysis run on three further datasets, and the result complicates rather than
cleanly closes this limitation: population median tortuosity sits consistently below the
Qwen3-0.6B range on all three (opposite direction from Apertus-8B's own single check,
which landed at/above it), while the class-specific archetype ({\tt<think>}/{\tt</think>})
still replicates directionally. Whether this is a model-scale effect (0.6B vs.\ 8B, same
family) or architecture-specific (differs from Apertus, a different family) is not yet
distinguishable with only one other 8B-scale model to compare against. Phi-4-mini-
reasoning has not yet had this analysis run at all. This -- not mere generation
completing -- is what would make a result in
\Cref{sec:results:geometry,sec:results:causalgeom} a cross-model claim rather than a
single-model one, and it is not yet in a stable enough state to write up as one.}

\textbf{Synthetic tasks.} Our six datasets are six rows in a replication table, not six
qualitatively different kinds of evidence -- they share the same templated generation
process and the same LLM annotator. MATH integration (\Cref{sec:math}) is in progress
specifically to address this.

\textbf{Category label reliability: checked, substantial overall, one category fragile.}
Our category labels were originally assigned without any inter-annotator agreement
statistic -- a limitation \citeauthor{thoughtanchors2025} share, reporting none for their
own labels either. We closed this with a genuinely independent second annotation (not a
re-run of the same labeling process, since the original labels were produced by manual
reading, not a callable procedure): a separate annotator, with no access to the original
labels, re-labeled a 106-sentence stratified subsample of \textsc{countries\_capitals}
(our best-powered dataset) from raw sentence text alone, using the identical published
taxonomy. Cohen's $\kappa=0.714$ (``substantial'' agreement, raw agreement 80/106) --
already ahead of \citeauthor{thoughtanchors2025}'s unreported figure. This aggregate
number understates how uneven reliability actually is: six of seven testable categories
show strong-to-perfect agreement (\texttt{uncertainty\_management} and
\texttt{final\_answer\_emission} both 15/15; \texttt{problem\_setup} and
\texttt{fact\_retrieval} both 14/15), but \texttt{result\_consolidation} shows
\textbf{0/15} -- every single instance was relabeled as something else, spread across
three other categories rather than concentrated on one confusable neighbor, indicating a
genuine boundary weakness in this specific category rather than one annotator's
idiosyncrasy. \texttt{active\_computation} remains untested (only one instance exists in
the entire 50-sample labeled set). This limitation applies to
\Cref{sec:results:position,sec:results:causal,sec:results:successor}, which use category
labels -- results resting specifically on \texttt{result\_consolidation} should be read
with more caution than the rest -- and does not apply to
\Cref{sec:results:geometry,sec:results:causalgeom}, which are entirely label-free.
\todo{A fully human-validated stratified subsample, rather than a second LLM annotator,
would make this stronger still; not yet done.}

\textbf{The founding premise (delta vs.\ state) is open, and we do not build on it.} We
tested whether tracking representational \emph{change} (our fingerprint object's
clustering-based validity, \Cref{sec:methods:fingerprint}) outperforms tracking raw
\emph{state} at recovering category structure, and found no evidence for it: across six
datasets and a symmetric neighbor-count sweep, 3 datasets favor delta, 1 is a coin flip,
and 2 -- including our best-powered dataset, with the single largest margin in the sweep
-- favor state. Two earlier passes at this comparison reported a clean delta win; both
were artifacts of an asymmetric sweep (holding the delta side fixed at whatever
neighbor-count value happened to already be on disk), which we caught and corrected
before treating either earlier result as real. We do not read this null as invalidating
the geometric results in \Cref{sec:results:geometry}: tortuosity, multi-lag persistence,
and growing distinctness are properties of a \emph{path}, which a state-only
representation cannot express by construction (there is no state-only analogue to
compare against, unlike the clustering-based category-recovery task this null result
concerns) -- but we do not claim deltas have been shown superior to state as a general
representational choice, and the paper's contribution rests on the geometric object's
own empirical properties, not on that claim.

\textbf{Our conditional-AMI estimator is an approximation.} Size-weighted within-bin AMI
(\Cref{sec:methods:position}) approximates $I(\text{cluster}; \text{category} \mid
\text{position})$; it is not the formal estimator, and we have not yet swept bin-edge
placement at fixed bin count or tried a continuous position covariate. (Raw AMI
magnitudes \emph{are} calibrated against a permutation null -- see
\Cref{sec:results:position}'s ``Calibration against a permutation null'' paragraph --
this approximation concerns the conditioning-on-position estimator itself, not whether
the reported AMI values are distinguishable from chance.)

\textbf{The bypass-vs-injection causal design is not a perfectly matched control.} Bypass
and injection compound differently through downstream layers -- both permit downstream
compensation, but the induced trajectories are not matched beyond the perturbation
magnitude at the intervention point itself. This does not undermine the \emph{direction}
of \Cref{tab:jointnecessity}'s result, which we verified survives a substantially harder
control than originally used, but the reported effect sizes should not be read as precise
estimates.

\section{Discussion and Conclusion}
\label{sec:discussion}

We show that a token's layer-wise representational trajectory -- not just its final
state -- is a real, measurably structured object: far straighter than chance at the
population level, with a specific, reproducible archetype for reasoning-delimiter tokens
that a purely static analysis has no way to express. We then asked the harder question of
whether this structure is causally load-bearing, not merely descriptive, and found a
genuinely mixed answer that we think is more useful reported honestly than smoothed over:
geometry does not predict causal importance locally, even under a causal target corrected
for a specific confound we identified along the way, but a real joint-necessity effect
exists among layers that are individually redundant, and it survives a substantially
harder control than the one we first used. We view stress-testing our own positive
result until it either broke or held -- rather than reporting the first version that
looked clean -- as part of the paper's contribution, not an afterthought.

Separately, we audited the standard tool this line of work uses to connect internal
state to CoT structure (sentence-category probing) and found it substantially confounded
by sequence position, verified this in the leading published result of this kind, and
quantified what survives proper control. We report this as a supporting, not central,
contribution: it explains why we build a label-free alternative rather than trusting
category probes as our primary instrument, and we think an interpretability paper's
obligation to report a confound in a widely-used tool is not diminished by also having a
positive result to lead with. We also chose to report, rather than quietly work around, a
null result on our own original methodological premise (change vs.\ state): our
best-powered dataset does not support it, and we build the paper's actual contribution on
the geometric object's directly measured properties rather than on that unresolved
premise.

\section{Impact Statement}

This paper presents work whose goal is to advance the interpretability of large language
model reasoning. Two points are worth practical note. First, our position-confound result
has a direct implication for downstream uses of sentence-category probes (e.g.\
monitoring or steering based on inferred reasoning-step category): practitioners should
control for position before trusting a probe's apparent sensitivity to reasoning content.
Second, our causal-geometry result -- that a group of individually-redundant layers can
be jointly necessary -- is a caution against inferring a component's importance from
single-component ablation studies alone, a common practice in interpretability and
model-editing work; joint or grouped interventions may be necessary to detect this class
of effect. We are not aware of other potential societal consequences of this work that
warrant specific discussion here.

\subsection{Causal-geometry hyperparameters}
Bypass/injection sweep: all 28 layers individually; pairwise and triple combinations for
